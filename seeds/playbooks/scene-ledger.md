# The Scene Ledger

Showing up is a strategy. Most paying music work in a city like OKC is booked
through relationships, and relationships are built in rooms — at art walks, open
mics, songwriter rounds, and festivals. The Scene Ledger turns "being around"
into something you can track, so the nights out actually compound into gigs.

## Where to be

The scene calendar (seeded, tagged **scene-ledger**) tracks the recurring rooms:

- **First Friday** on the Paseo — 25+ galleries, live music, the whole scene in
  one night.
- **Open mics and songwriter rounds** — the **Blue Note** and **JJ's Alley**
  open mics, plus whatever the patrol list surfaces. Open mics at venues that
  *hire* are worth more than random ones.
- **The Blue Door** — the best listening room in Oklahoma for singer-songwriters;
  the room where an originals set builds a real audience.
- **Festivals + their application windows** — Festival of the Arts, Downtown
  Edmond, Norman Arts Festival. Booking runs through application windows, so the
  calendar tracks when to apply, not just when to show up.

## The ledger part

Showing up only pays if you write it down. After each event, log **who you met**
in the people log (M6) — booker, worship leader, planner, fellow musician — and
where. Referral tracking then shows which relationships actually produce gigs,
so you spend more nights in the rooms that convert and fewer in the ones that
don't.

## Stepping stones, ranked honestly

Open mics, showcases, and competitions are **stepping stones**, never paid work
— they live on the Stepping-stones shelf and never sort above a paying gig. A
stepping stone earns its place only through strategic value: a cash-prize
competition (see the seeded **Kerrville New Folk**, **John Lennon**, and other
songwriting contests), a showcase with real bookers in the room, or an open mic
at a venue known to hire. Show up on purpose.
