# The Sub List

Churches and bands constantly need last-minute subs on keys. A worship leader
whose pianist is sick on Saturday night is not running a search — they are
texting everyone they know at 9pm. The goal of this playbook is simple: **be on
that text.**

## Why subbing is the long game

Sub work is how you become the next hire. You get in the room, you prove you can
read a chart and follow a leader, and when the regular seat opens — or when that
leader moves to a bigger church — you are the known quantity. One reliable
Sunday can become a standing position.

## Build the one-page sub sheet

From the vault, keep a single **sub sheet**: your keys/vocals, the styles you
cover (worship, hymns, gospel, contemporary), your gear, and "available this
Sunday" contact. One tap should draft an availability note — Vamp drafts it, you
send it.

## The contact list

Seeded churches (**First Presbyterian**, **Crossings**, **St. Paul's
Cathedral**, **Life.Church**, plus Overpass-imported congregations) are tagged
**sub-list**. Work toward the **music director / worship leader** for each —
that's the person who texts the sub call. (Confirm the name; never invent one.)

## Seasonal blitz

Demand spikes before **Christmas and Easter**, when every church adds services
and their regulars burn out. This playbook activates in March–April and
November–December: send a short "I'm available for the season" note to the whole
sub list ahead of the rush. Funeral homes (also tagged sub-list) run on the same
on-call logic year-round.
