# The Dormant Piano Tour

A venue that owns a piano nobody plays is a pre-qualified lead. The capital cost
is already sunk — there is a real instrument sitting in a lobby, a dining room,
or a ballroom, depreciating in silence. You are not asking them to buy anything.
You are offering to make an asset they already own start earning.

## Why it works

Most cold pitches ask a venue to imagine live music from scratch: where would it
go, what would it cost, is there room. A dormant piano answers all three
questions before you open your mouth. The pitch writes itself: *"Your piano
could be earning."*

## The tour

Seeded prospects tagged **dormant-piano-tour** are hotels, upscale restaurants
and steakhouses, and country clubs — the venue types most likely to own an
underused piano. For each, the first job is **confirmation**, not a pitch:

1. Confirm the piano exists and isn't already programmed. (Several rows are
   `verified: false` with the piano flagged unknown — do not assume.)
2. If it's played (like the Skirvin's **Red Piano Lounge**), the play is to join
   the rotation, not to cold-start it — those are tagged toward the scene ledger.
3. If it's dormant, pitch a standing weekly or monthly cocktail/dinner set.

## The pitch

*"I noticed you have a beautiful piano in the [lobby/dining room]. I'd love to
make it part of your [Friday evenings]. Here's a short video of exactly the vibe
I'd bring."* Bring proof (the live-video asset from your vault) and a specific
slot, not an open-ended offer.

## The small-city rule

Only pitch a dormant piano you'd be proud to play in public. Every message is
one you'd stand behind at an open mic — that reputation is the whole game in a
metro this size.
